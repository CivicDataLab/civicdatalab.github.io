context: |
  As data increasingly shapes governance, public policy, business, healthcare, education, and everyday decision-making, the ability to understand and critically engage with data has become an essential skill, not just for technologists, but for everyone. Yet, most data literacy resources assume prior technical knowledge, creating barriers for students and professionals from non-technical backgrounds.

  Beyond statistics and dashboards, data is deeply intertwined with human values, institutions, and power. How data is collected, interpreted, governed, and used influences everything from public services and AI systems to privacy, inclusion, and accountability. Building a broader understanding of data is therefore essential for enabling informed participation in an increasingly digital society.
solution: |
  To bridge this gap, CivicDataLab developed Understanding Data: Practical Foundations for Non-Tech Learners, an interactive online course designed to make data concepts accessible, engaging, and relevant for learners without a technical background.

  The course introduces learners to the complete data lifecycle, covering how data is collected, cleaned, analysed, visualised, governed, and applied in real-world contexts. Moving beyond technical concepts, it encourages participants to critically examine how data reflects societal values, shapes governance, influences decision-making, and raises important ethical and policy questions.

  Designed with an interactive learning approach, the course combines flip cards, comparison tables, practical scenarios, strategy tools, and visual explainers to simplify complex ideas. Learners develop foundational skills in data collection, preparation, basic statistical analysis, and visualisation while also building a critical understanding of data governance, privacy, bias, and responsible data use.

  By the end of the course, participants are able to:

  - Understand the complete data lifecycle, from collection and cleaning to analysis and visualisation.
  - Develop practical data literacy skills without requiring coding knowledge.
  - Examine how data is shaped by human values, assumptions, and power structures.
  - Analyse real-world challenges related to data governance, privacy, ethics, and public policy.
  - Build a responsible and critical approach to engaging with data across professional and civic contexts.

  Upon successful completion, learners earn a recognised micro-credential. The course is particularly suited for students across disciplines - especially those from non-technical backgrounds as well as early-career professionals, educators, researchers, civil society practitioners, and anyone seeking to understand the growing role of data in society.
