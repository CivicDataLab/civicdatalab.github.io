name: Coastal and Ocean Resilience Data Collaborative (CORD)
summary: Strengthening Infrastructure and Safety in Vulnerable Coastal Regions of India
context: >-
  India’s long coastline is home to millions of people, critical infrastructure,
  rich ecosystems and economic activity, but is increasingly exposed to
  cyclones, flooding, coastal erosion, rising sea levels and other
  climate-related risks. Fragmented coastal data across government departments
  and sectors, limited interoperability and gaps in cross-sector coordination
  constrain risk-informed planning, resource mobilisation and long-term
  resilience.

  To address this, we are developing the Coastal & Ocean Resilience Data Collaborative (CORD), an interoperable open data platform and a collaborative framework for resilient coastal planning. The CORD is being developed with support from the[ Lloyd’s Register Foundation](http://www.lrfoundation.org.uk), focusing on vulnerable coastal regions across Odisha, Tamil Nadu and Kerala.
solution: >
  CORD will bring together government, researchers and other changemakers across
  coastal resilience to better understand the interconnected risks coastal
  communities face. The platform will connect and standardise fragmented
  datasets on coastal hazards, infrastructure, communities, environmental
  conditions, public finance, while incorporating local knowledge and community
  participation. By applying advanced analytics, AI and Machine Learning (ML),
  and building institutional capacity to help decision-makers and practitioners
  identify vulnerabilities and resource gaps, understand interconnected risks,
  prioritise interventions and mobilise investments for climate resilience. It
  will also support institutional capacity building to strengthen the use of
  data in planning and decision-making.


  Designed as an open-source, modular platform, CORD’s architecture and approaches can be adapted to different coastal contexts. By bringing together diverse data, analytical tools and local knowledge, it aims to support more informed, participatory and locally grounded approaches to coastal resilience.


  Earlier in 2026, [CORD was endorsed as a project under UN Ocean Practices for the Decade Programme](https://www.ioc.unesco.org/en/articles/ocean-decade-endorsement-accelerates-partnerships-and-data-sharing-coastal-resilience), joining a global movement of science-based initiatives working toward the "science we need for the ocean we want."
